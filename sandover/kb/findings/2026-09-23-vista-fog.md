## Answer (≤5 lines)
All three policies plus a first `unified` model now run behind `pc-set-vista-fog`. Captioned
sheets for three viewpoints at noon and dusk are in `$SANDOVER_DATA/shots/vista-fog/`
(`sheet-hut.png`, `sheet-high.png`, `sheet-misty.png`). Vista off is pixel-identical to master
(0 px on all six shots). The fog policy does not change the cost measurably: the vista draw is
0.9-1.6 ms whatever the policy.

## Evidence (path:line@sha / URL, short excerpts)
Code (branch `p4/vista-fog`):
- `pc-set-vista-fog <mode> a b c` (`game/kernel/common/kmachine.cpp`, Jak 1 block;
  `goal_src/jak1/kernel-defs.gc`). Modes: `vanilla` (the vista uses the viewer's fog),
  `push_out` (a = fog end in m, for every background draw), `own` (a = fog end in m, vista
  only) and `unified` (a = start in m, b = density in 0.001/km, c = scale height in m).
  ImGui: Renderer Debug → Vista.
- `push_out` and `unified` are applied in `first_tfrag_draw_setup`
  (`background/background_common.cpp`), which every tfrag, tie, etie and shrub draw goes
  through. They are active only while the vista is enabled; `render_state->vista_frame_fog`
  is set per frame in `dispatch_buckets_jak1`, so other games never see it.
- `unified` fog: `od = density * max(d - start, 0) * exp(-max(y_mid, 0) / H)` and
  `fog = (255 - fog_min) * (1 - exp(-od))`, with `d` the vertex's distance from the camera
  in m and `y_mid` the mean height of camera and vertex. It uses the level's mood fog colour
  and keeps the level's maximum fog (`fog_min`). It is in `tfrag3.vert`, `shrub.vert`,
  `etie_base.vert` and `tie_wind.vert`. Wind ties are instanced and have no world position in
  the shader, so they use view depth and the camera's height instead.
- Stand-ins: `=name` tokens in `pc-set-vista-hide` match a texture or proto name exactly. The
  village2 runs hide exactly `v1-lores-vil2-wall` and `v1-lores-vil2-canvas`; the vista facts
  `vil1-medres-wood` is kept, so there is no ground hole. The misty island is a
  `med-res-level` actor, not background, so the misty runs kill it with a rig stage hook
  (`*rig-stage-hook*`, run after the settle frames).
- Worktree launch: gk reads its shaders from the project root, so a worktree build needs its
  own `game/` next to the main data. The rig now takes `SANDOVER_GK_PROJ_PATH` (gk only; goalc
  and `(mi)` stay on `$SANDOVER_ROOT`). That root is a directory of junctions to the main
  checkout, with `game` pointing at the worktree (`howto/rig-capture.md`).

Captures: manifest `sandover/shots/vista-fog.yaml`, 2560x1440 MSAA 4, Jak hidden, effects,
wind and particles off. One directory per policy: `off` (vista off), `vanilla`, `push_out`
(6000 m), `own` (6000 m), `unified-a` (64 m, 0.35/km, H 300 m) and `unified-b` (64 m, 0.9/km,
no height falloff).

| view | camera | vista |
|---|---|---|
| hut | 110 m above the village1 hut, toward village2 | village2, about 1.9 km |
| high | the vista facts' 450 m vantage over village1 | village2 |
| misty | village1 shore toward misty island | misty, about 0.7 km |

My read of the sheets (the author's opinion, for the maintainer to overrule):
- `vanilla`: the real level is there but is almost pure fog colour. The only visible change
  is that the stand-in walls are gone.
- `push_out`: coherent, but the whole level loses its haze. The sea, hills and horizon skirts
  go sharp, which changes the level's look far more than the vista does. From misty, a
  nearby palm also pops in.
- `own`: the vista reads as pasted on: crisp inside a hazy level, and a dark cut-out at dusk.
- `unified` A: the vista and terrain fade together. The height falloff makes high views
  nearly as clear as `push_out`.
- `unified` B: the most natural of the set. The vista stays readable and the stock haze and
  dusk depth are kept.
- A unified model with a density between A and B and a weak height term looks like the right
  direction. Tuning is left for after the pick.

Cost (GPU timestamps, 240-frame averages, ms, 2560x1440 MSAA 4, solo with no other gk running;
`cost.json` next to the sheets):

| view | buckets, vista off | buckets, vista on | vista-draw, all policies |
|---|---|---|---|
| high | 0.39 | 1.48-2.14 | 0.99-1.60 |
| hut | 0.91 | 2.02-2.29 | 0.96-1.35 |
| misty | 0.40 | 1.33-1.50 | 0.90-1.00 |

The spread between policies is within the run-to-run noise. The extra per-vertex `exp` in
`unified` does not show.

Parity: `parity-base/` (worktree built at master) against `off/` (branch, vista off), six
shots: 0 px differ in every shot.

## Implications for the design
- Picking a policy is now purely an art call; none of the three costs anything measurable.
- `unified` only covers background (tfrag, tie, shrub). Ocean, merc, generic and sprites keep
  the mood fog. The ocean is drawn in screen space and has no world position, so it needs its
  own path before `unified` can replace per-level fog everywhere.
- Some stand-ins are actors (`med-res-level`), not background. The stand-in manifest
  must cover actors as well as tfrag and tie names.
- The misty vista renders almost black: the viewer's palette weights (`itimes`) are applied to
  misty's colour sets. The palette axis matters more than the vista facts assumed.
- Black gaps in the high and hut views are there with the vista off too. They are the clear
  colour behind village1's geometry, not a vista artefact.
