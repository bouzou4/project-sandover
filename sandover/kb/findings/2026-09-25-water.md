## Answer (≤5 lines)
Yes, for the near and mid ocean. The wave normal comes from the gradient of the animated
ocean texture (no procedural waves were needed). On top: sun light on the wave slopes,
crest sparkle, a Blinn glint and fresnel toward the mood fog colour. Default `subtle`
preset. Cost about 0.06 ms GPU at 1440p with MSAA 4. `pc-set-water`, off by default; the
off path is the vanilla shader code.

## Evidence
- Engine: `ocean_common.frag` `water_shade`, `Materials.cpp` `water_setup_ocean`,
  `CommonOceanRenderer.cpp` (near and mid flush). How-to: `kb/howto/lighting.md` §7.
- View ray: the ocean vertices are GS screen coordinates. The shader inverts the game's
  projection (`gs - hvdf = fog * p_xx * x / (p_zw * z)`) and rotates by the camera. The
  normal is world up. Debug view 4 (V) points up and toward the camera in both shots:
  grazing at the beach, steep from the temple top.
- Sun: the blended fitted day sun that the class shading also uses. The ocean gets it
  through `water_note_background` (TFragment) when PBR is off. At beach-start noon the sun
  is near the zenith; at temple-top dusk it is low and behind the camera. So neither shot
  can show a real mirror glint: it would land at the viewer's feet in the first and outside
  the view in the second. Seen in debug view 2 at exponent 2. That is why the preset adds
  crest sparkle. The crests are texels brighter than the average of their surroundings,
  taken from the texture and a rotated, finer copy of it. They are lit by the sun and
  stronger toward the half vector.
- Bloom: the glint and sparkle write the emissive mask during the base ocean pass, so bloom
  picks up peaks above the threshold. On the FP16 target, colour above 1 is kept.
- Captures, 2560x1440, MSAA 4, with SSAO contact, bloom, actor shadows, PBR maps and the
  FP16 target on: `$SANDOVER_DATA/shots/ocean/{final-off,final-on}`, manifest
  `sandover/shots/ocean.yaml`.
- Cost: one solo run at beach-start noon, `ocean-mid` GPU timer (`SSAO-TIMING`), two
  240-frame windows per state. Off 0.118 / 0.128 ms, on 0.176 / 0.178 ms, so about
  +0.055 ms. The near ocean does not draw in that shot. `$SANDOVER_DATA/shots/ocean/cost.json`.

## Parity
Not run for this PR (repo rule: parity is a milestone check). With the toggle off, the
shader takes the `water_on == 0` branch, which is the unchanged vanilla code. The only
extra GL work is one `water_on` uniform per flush. The emissive draw buffers stay as
vanilla leaves them, and `compute_frame_lights` is a pure refactor of the class-shading sun
code.

## Implications for the design
- Ocean-far (direct renderer, the band at the horizon) is unshaded. Under fog it meets the
  mid ocean without a visible seam in the temple shot.
- At night (no sidecar sun), only the wave normal and fresnel apply. A moon glint would need
  the key light.
- The mid ocean draws before the background, so it uses the previous frame's camera
  and sun.
