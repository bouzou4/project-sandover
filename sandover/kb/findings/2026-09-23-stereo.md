## Answer (≤5 lines)
Yes. Replaying the frame's DMA chain once per eye, with the eye offset folded into each renderer's camera, gives two coherent eyes. Disparity is 0 at the sky and grows toward the camera. Jak matches the ground he stands on. With 64 mm IPD at 1920 px per eye, disparity is 5-11 px on the ground and 0 at the sky. Background, merc, generic, 3D sprites and glow take the offset in their camera math. Ocean and shadow arrive as GS screen coordinates and need a depth-derived shift, which is now in. Still wrong: HUD/text (zero parallax), sprite distort, GOAL-side actor culling at diorama scales, and the cost (the whole dispatch runs twice). With stereo off the build is pixel-identical to master at village1-hut, and within run-to-run noise at the other two spots.

## Evidence
**How it works** (`game/graphics/opengl_renderer/OpenGLRenderer.cpp:1495@0ad51e600`, `dispatch_buckets_stereo`)
- `dispatch_buckets` takes the `DmaFollower` by value, so the frame can be dispatched twice.
- Eye 0 is rendered, blitted into the left half of a 2w×h single-sample FBO, then the render FBO is cleared and eye 1 is rendered into the right half.
- The window blit and internal-resolution screenshots read that FBO. `pc-screen-shot` gives a 2w×h side-by-side PNG.
- Pass 2 skips `vif_interrupt_callback` (it calls into GOAL). It does not bump `frame_idx`, and it restores the tie wind springs to their start-of-frame state (`Tie3.cpp:379@0ad51e600`).
- Eye offset e = ±IPD/2 × 4096 / world_scale game units. Camera-space +x is screen left (measured: the first sign tried gave negative disparity for near objects), so the left eye uses +e.

| Renderer | Where the eye goes | Status |
|---|---|---|
| tfrag, tie (incl. wind, etie), shrub | `apply_stereo_eye` on the PC port data when it is read. It moves `trans` along camera x, sets `camera[3] -= e*persp[0]` and moves the frustum `planes` (`background_common.cpp:818`) | works; culling exact per eye |
| merc, emerc | `perspective[3] -= e*perspective[0]` (`Merc2.cpp:904`) | works (Jak feet 6.7 px, sand beside 7-8 px) |
| generic | `(position - (e,0,0)) * scale` in `generic.vert:50`; 0 for HUD draws | inferred (no generic-specific probe) |
| sprite 3D/billboard | world-to-clip translation row (`Sprite3.cpp:219`) | works (eco-vent fx 36.0 px vs ground 34.8 px, diorama) |
| glow | camera-space translation before its separate perspective (`Sprite3_Glow.cpp:190`) | works (same fx) |
| ocean near/mid/far, shadow | GS x += k·(z − z0), k and z0 from the frame's perspective, hvdf and fog (`BucketRenderer.cpp:98`) | works (see below) |
| sky | nothing: zero disparity is correct for parallel eyes | correct (clouds 0.00 px) |

On the C++ side, every new path is behind `stereo_active` or `eye != 0`.

## Merc models to glTF (side question)
Yes, the existing tooling does this. Set `"rip_levels": true` in `decompiler/config/jak1/jak1_config.jsonc`, or use `--config-override '{"rip_levels": true}'` on a `levels_extract` run. `extract_level.cpp` then writes the following under `decompiler_out/jak1/levels/`:
- `<level>/<level>-background.glb`
- one `<model>.glb` per merc model, in the level's own folder.
- `common/<model>.glb` for merc models in the shared data (Jak lives there).

`add_merc` (`decompiler/level_extractor/fr3_to_gltf.cpp:1656`) exports positions, UVs, vertex colours and normals. It also exports skin weights and joints with inverse bind matrices, every animation of the matching art group, and blerc morph targets. Textures are embedded, because `rip_levels` keeps them in the level data. This is enough for a review viewer. Merc vertex lighting is not baked in; the colours are the raw vertex colours.

## Implications for the design
- For OpenXR, keep this structure: pass an eye offset (later a full per-eye view and asymmetric projection) through `SharedRenderState` and replay the chain per eye. Only GS-space renderers need the depth-derived shift. A full per-eye rotation (head tracking) would break that shortcut: the shift is exact only for a translation parallel to the image plane. Ocean and shadow would then need their pre-projection data, or a reprojection.
- Diorama presets make GOAL-side culling matter. Widen the `*math-camera*` planes before the first diorama preset ships.
- The HUD cannot stay in the eye buffers. This confirms the quad-layer decision in ADR-0004.
