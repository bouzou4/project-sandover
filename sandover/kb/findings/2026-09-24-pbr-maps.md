## Answer (≤5 lines)
Yes, as a stylized first pass. `sandover-materials derive-maps` (recipes v2) derives normal and
roughness maps for all 4002 textures in about 70 s (logical threads - 2 workers). The contact sheets read as
faithful to the painted detail: carved lines on stone and precursor, grain on wood, faint
scratches on metal, soft relief on organic and cloth. Not yet judged in game; the engine still
uses procedural detail normals (`2026-09-24-pbr-spike.md`).

## Evidence
- Run: 4002 textures, 3456 from the alpha-normalized 4x pack and 546 vanilla only; 55 s for
  v1, 69 s for v2 (extra band on stone and precursor). Index and maps under
  `$SANDOVER_DATA/materials/pbr-maps/`, sheets under `pbr-maps/sheets/`.
- Viewer: `pack + derived maps` set in PBR preview, checked headless on
  `sunken/sun-iris-door-lod0.glb` (1/1 primitive mapped, metallic, envMap gradient).
- Pack vs vanilla as the source (35 non-flat pack textures, 6 per class): mean angle between
  the pack-derived normal and the vanilla-derived normal upsampled 4x is 1.35 deg; the pack
  gives sharper edges (slope p95 8.5 vs 6.1 deg) and puts 14% of its normal energy above the
  vanilla Nyquist band. Side by side the shapes match; the pack's lines are crisper, with no
  invented features at the recipe sigmas (band_lo >= 0.35 vanilla texels). Kept pack as the
  default, `--source vanilla` as the option.
- Known weak spots: decal-like organic textures (hard colour edges, e.g. `rat-ear`) emboss
  their colour boundaries; alpha-tested cutouts are flat outside the mask by design.

## Research (three parallel web-research lanes, summarised)
- **Algorithmic**: Materialize's height-from-diffuse is a weighted pyramid of Gaussian bands
  on gamma luminance with contrast and bias terms. Adopted as `extra_bands` (a second, broader
  band at low weight on stone and precursor). Delighting has no published formula beyond
  high-passing broad luminance; the recipe's widest band already does that. Extending the
  structure-tensor grain to cloth (weave) and foliage (veins) is an open option.
  https://github.com/BoundingBoxSoftware/Materialize
- **AI alternatives (optional, per class)**: PBRify_Remix 1x normal/roughness models (CC0,
  ESRGAN-sized, run locally, seconds per texture, no class prior) as a generic baseline;
  Ubisoft CHORD (research-only licence, SD-scale, text prompt usable as the class prior)
  as the likeliest to beat filters on metal, precursor and lava; StableMaterials (OpenRAIL,
  512 px, text+image) for stone, wood, cloth; Material Palette too slow for 4000 (per-image
  fine-tune); Marigold-IID out of distribution on flat-shaded PS2 art. All diffusion methods
  risk photoreal micro-detail against the art direction.
  https://github.com/Kim2091/PBRify_Remix, https://github.com/ubisoft/ubisoft-laforge-chord,
  https://huggingface.co/gvecchio/StableMaterials
- **Low-res game textures**: PBRify derives at 1x, separate from the upscale; the Painkiller
  RTX write-up hand-authors metallic, treats roughness as the main quality lever and keeps
  height for large flat surfaces only. SurfaceNet uses a material-class prior, as the recipes
  do. https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-painkiller-rtx-uses-generative-ai-to-modernize-game-assets-at-scale,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11298

## Implications for the design
- The algorithmic path is the default; tuning is a TOML edit (`pbr-recipes.toml`) and a
  one-minute rerun. AI models are alternatives per class, not a replacement.
- Metallic is a class scalar (metal and precursor 1): consistent with hand-authored metallic.
- The engine needs these maps bound per texture to replace its procedural detail normals.

## Follow-ups
