# Vista neighbour cost, and why a distance LOD does not cut it (opt/vista, 2026-10-10 to 10-15)

The 2026-10-10 profile put the vista neighbours at up to 15 ms a frame at the sea and high spots
(`2026-10-10-perf-profile.md`, `vista` marginal). This pass measured where that time goes and
tried a distance LOD for the neighbour draws. The LOD made frames slower, and an exact CPU trim
did not prove itself either, so no engine code lands: this note is the result.

## Setup

- Timing: `sandover-perf` on `sandover/shots/perf-max.yaml` (msaa 2 plus SMAA, max tier,
  `wait_log_timeout` 180, every spot's neighbour wait completed), 45-frame windows, solo GPU, both
  build lanes held from before boot. Arm A (`perf/opt-vista-final2-A`): the staged play build of
  c33058c53, not current master (two later changes merged after it). Arm B
  (`perf/opt-vista-final2-B`): c33058c53 plus the CPU trims below. Both ran from one staged
  project root with one `SANDOVER_DATA`, one `--toggles vista` window set per spot (one base
  window, so no noise band).
- Diagnostic split runs (`$SANDOVER_DATA/perf/opt-vista-diag1` .. `diag7`): msaa 4 plus SMAA as
  the profile, base and each probe in one session (A/B/A). `diag6` is invalid: three of its spots
  rendered at 320x240 (the display was off). Probes were dev-only vista options on a branch
  build; none ship.

About 60 % of the vista time is fragment work on pixels the neighbours really cover. At
open-sea-low the scissor saves 8.9 ms of the 14.7 ms that TIE, tfrag and shrub off save together
(diag1). Half-screen scissors put that cost on the visible islands and hills, not under the sea
or behind the viewer level. Overdraw is an estimated 2x: GL_SAMPLES_PASSED counted 8.55 M
msaa-4 samples at open-sea-low (tfrag 2.09, TIE 5.82, shrub 0.64; diag4), against an estimated
4-5 M samples of coverage (the islands fill about 12-14 % of the frame by eye, 1.0-1.2 M pixels).
The rest is vertex, raster and per-draw work. A neighbour pixel costs what a viewer-level pixel
costs; nothing is wrong with the vista path itself.

This agrees with the deep-opt note's rejection of a vista depth prepass (row 83, vertex/submit
bound). A prepass draws all 1400-2300 vista draws twice, and at the per-draw cost below that
outweighs halving a 2x overdraw.

## Distance LOD: tried, slower

- Geometry: TIE geometry 1 has as many triangles as geometry 0 and geometry 2 about 90 %; only
  geometry 3 (about 27 %) is a real LOD. Tfrag geometry 2 is 20-30 %.
- Projected size: no visible TIE or tfrag vis node (a TIE node holds 1-8 instances) projects
  under 4 px radius at 4K. Almost all are 16-64 px or more: 500 m to 2 km away, a node is still
  tens of pixels. A vis-node cull under 1 or 0.5 px removed no draw and no triangle at any spot
  (diag1). This is compatible with deep-opt bit 11, which cuts shrub strips under a pixel inside
  wider chunks: shrubs are not in these node stats, and bit 11 is already in the base.
- Geometry 3 / tfrag 2 for nodes below 16, 32 or 64 px radius (diag2) removed up to 1.2 M of
  3.4 M triangles at open-sea-low. Each tree is then drawn twice, once per LOD: +35-76 draws at
  16 px, +106-315 at 32 px, +80-335 at 64 px. Frame time:
  - 16 px: up to 1.4 ms slower; beach-shore 0.4 ms faster.
  - 32 px: 0.5-3.2 ms slower at every spot.
  - 64 px: 0.8-2.0 ms slower, except temple-top (+0.5, inside its noise).

  The triangles were never the cost. The lower LODs also have no lighting sidecar; a
  nearest-vertex transfer was written and works, but does not change the verdict.
- Per-draw cost, estimated as the 32 and 64 px frame loss over the extra draws: 4-6 us at
  beach-shore and open-sea-low, 5-8 us at hut-roof, 9-16 us at open-sea-high, and up to about
  18 us at noisy temple-top. Draw count alone does not explain the loss at open-sea-high; the
  rest is unexplained.
- Seabed cull (nodes wholly under the opaque water depth): nothing; the fragment cost is above
  the water line.
- TIE before tfrag within a level: 0.0-0.7 ms slower.
- Per-level cheaper shading (no PBR, no shadow terms) for neighbours whose nearest point is
  beyond 300 m: 4.0-5.1 ms at open-sea-low only (village1 is 300-600 m out there), nothing at the
  other spots (some neighbour geometry is always within 300 m). It is a visible change (PBR is the
  material look) and is left to a visual judgement call; see Next.

## Exact CPU trims: tried, not shipped

The trims, vista only:
- upload a neighbour tree's time-of-day colours only when its palette weights change (they
  depend on the itimes alone);
- skip the per-tree setup of a TIE category with no visible draw;
- cull a tfrag tree before its setup, and skip the setup when nothing is in view.

A saving was not demonstrated.

- Same-session probe (msaa 4, `perf/opt-vista-diag3`, trims on / off / on). Vista CPU ms with the
  trims off, against the mean of the windows either side:

  | spot | trims off | trims on (mean) |
  |---|---|---|
  | beach-shore | 4.98 | 3.76 |
  | open-sea-low | 6.55 | 5.10 |
  | open-sea-high | 3.74 | 3.86 |
  | hut-roof | 4.31 | 4.45 |

  So off read 1.2 and 1.45 ms higher at two spots and slightly lower at the other two. The
  windows also carry single spike frames of 22-51 ms, which move a 45-frame mean by about 0.5 ms.
- Final A/B (`perf/opt-vista-final2-A` vs `-B`, back to back under one lane hold, same root):
  - vista CPU fell 0.1-0.5 ms at 6 of 7 spots and rose 5.5 -> 7.3 ms at open-sea-low;
  - the vista GPU group read 0.1-1.3 ms higher in B;
  - whole frames moved -1.2..+3.9 ms, with one base window per spot.

  Two more confounds sat between the arms: the tools checkout moved (fb1b823c0 for A, e747b7904
  for B), and CPU `present` time changed from 4.5 to 1.3-2.2 ms.

Not shipped.

Trap found on the way: the first cut skipped the texture bind with the upload, and tfrag then
drew with whatever time-of-day texture unit 10 held (village1's tower from the sea bright and
unfogged, `vista-lod-1/diff-final/open-sea-low-crop1-*`). The draw relies on the
bind the upload leaves; any later attempt must bind every time.

Perf-harness spot frames are not pixel-stable between runs (wind sway, Jak's pose, the beach
cliff's light at beach-shore), so a look A/B for vista work needs the paused rig capture, not
the frames `sandover-perf` writes.

Pan at hut-roof (`vista-lod-1/pan-A`, `pan-B`; 24 frames, 0.5 deg and 8 m toward the
view per frame, msaa 2): with no LOD there is nothing to pop. The frames of the two builds differ
only in palm sway (wind clock), 0.6-2.9 % of pixels over 8 per frame, none on the neighbours.

  of the profile are vista neighbour draws and first-use costs. They are not the world prepass
  (`world-cpu` 0.7-1.4 ms) and not the tile lights.
  - Vista neighbour re-entry, peak frame ms (`vista-cpu-ms`):
    - village2 cold pan with tile lights off: 57.7 (52.4);
    - jungle base windows right after warm pans: 59.0 (52.7) and 57.4 (49.2), recurring each
      time the camera returns to the rig view;
    - village2 timing pan: 64.2 (58.0).
  - Unattributed first-use frames, with `vista-cpu` small: jungle 58.2 (2.1) and village2 63.8
    (1.0), on the first pan only.

  No engine change from this pass touches the re-entry path. Next vista item: split the re-entry
  frame (renderer setup, wind matrices, chunk builds, multidraw lists) with a CPU scope per stage.
- The exact CPU trims above: if retried, ship them only behind an in-session A/B (on / off / on)
  that shows a saving outside the window spikes. No saving is demonstrated so far.
- Visual judgement calls, not imperceptible:
  - PBR off for neighbour pixels beyond a fog or distance threshold: 3.7 ms of PBR at the sea and
    high spots, but it needs a per-pixel fade, not the per-level switch measured here.
  - Shadow terms off for neighbours beyond the world map's reach: 1.7-1.8 ms, but the term is not
    an identity outside the map, so it changes colour.
- Draw-call cost: an estimated 4-16 us of GPU per vista draw (above). Merging the per-texture
  draws of a tree needs bindless or array textures.
