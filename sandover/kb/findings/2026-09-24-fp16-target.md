## Answer (≤5 lines)
`pc-set-hdr-target` switches the Jak 1 main colour target to `RGBA16F`; the final pass tonemaps
(clamp, or a knee/shoulder filmic curve). Toggle off passes parity (16/16, worst FLIP 0.0029).
Toggle on with clamp first failed 12/16 (FLIP ≈ 0.11 on every outdoor shot) because of the sky;
saturating the GS-emulation (DirectRenderer) draws brings it to 15/16. The one failure left is
village1 noon (0.033): an alpha-blended dirt path over grass that is above 1.0.

"Pixels > 1.0" comes from the `hdr-stats` line (sky saturated, before the tonemap). The
brightest channel reaches 2.3 to 3.6 outdoors at noon and dusk and 1.2 in sunken. At the end of
the frame there are no non-finite pixels and at most 0.001% negative ones.

Causes, from the FLIP maps, crops and above-1 masks:

- **Sky (fixed).** On every outdoor shot the error was the sky. The gradient goes above 1.0
  and the cloud layer darkens it. On `RGBA8` the gradient is clipped to 1 first, so the clouds
  read as grey on blue; on `RGBA16F` they stay above 1 and clip to white. The sky is drawn by
  `DirectRenderer`; its two shaders now saturate their output while the target is FP16
  (`saturate_out`), which reproduces `RGBA8` for every GS-emulation draw (sky, HUD, debug).
- **Translucent layers over overbright opaque (inherent).** village1 noon: the dirt path is
  alpha-blended tfrag over grass at 1.3 to 2.3; blending with the unclamped grass keeps the
  path brighter than vanilla. The same mechanism, smaller, on beach and jungle at noon (0.006
  to 0.008). This is what FP16 means; removing it would need a saturate on tfrag output, which
  also removes the headroom.
- **Sunken (not FP16).** The error is inside the window: ambient fish actors differ between
  runs (the toggle-off run shows a different fish from the golden); the FP16 runs agree with
  each other within 0.0005 there.
- **Filmic.** The knee at 0.8 also compresses 0.8 to 1.0, so noon sand and grass become paler
  and less saturated (the shoulder is per channel). FLIP rises only where much of the frame is
  above 0.8; dawn, night and sunken are unchanged.

Source alpha above 1.0 was tested by clamping alpha at the output of the tfrag, tie, shrub,
generic, merc, ocean and sprite shaders (`$SANDOVER_DATA/experiments/p2-fp16/alpha`): no
measurable change (every shot within 0.001 of the run without it), so it was not kept.

Cost (solo, one instance, noon, 2560x1440 MSAA 4, 240-frame GPU timer windows,
`$SANDOVER_DATA/experiments/p2-fp16/cost.json`): the bucket timer goes from 1.6 to 2.4 ms
(village1), 1.1 to 1.3 ms (beach), 1.9 to 2.3 ms (jungle); an off-to-off repeat varies by up to
0.6 ms, so read it as a few tenths of a millisecond. The final-pass timer includes the wait for
the swap and does not isolate the tonemap. Colour memory doubles (1440p MSAA 4: about 59 to
118 MB, plus the resolve buffer).

## Audited passes (read destination alpha or rely on saturation)

| Pass | Mechanism | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Sky (`DirectRenderer`, bucket `sky`) | gradient above 1, clouds rely on the clip | Fixed: output saturated under FP16 |
| Other `DirectRenderer` draws (HUD, progress, debug, sky-blend textures) | GS saturating writes | Saturated under FP16 (same switch) |
| `SRC_0_DST_DST` (`Cs·Ad + Cd`) in `background_common`, `DirectRenderer`, `Generic2`, ocean envmap (`CommonOceanRenderer` bucket 2) | reads destination alpha in PS2 units (0x80 = 1.0, stored as 0.5 with `color_mult` 0.5) | Unchanged. A writer with alpha above 1.0 would double the term; the alpha-clamp test found no effect on the shot set. Ocean writes `a·2` (`ocean_common.vert`): watch ocean shots |
| `SRC_ALPHA / ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA` with source alpha above 1 | `RGBA8` clamps the factor; float gives a negative destination weight | Tested, no measurable effect; not clamped |
| `REVERSE_SUBTRACT` (`Cd − Cs·As`): background, direct, generic | can go below 0 on float and absorb later adds | End-of-frame negatives ≤ 0.001% of pixels; not fixed |
| Jak 1 shadow (`ShadowRenderer`, `DST_COLOR` multiply) | multiplies unclamped ground: shadows on overbright ground stay brighter | Inherent; part of the noon residue |
| Alpha blending over opaque above 1 (tfrag/tie translucent) | blends with the unclamped value | Inherent; the village1 noon failure |
| Sprite distort, `FramebufferCopier` (warp) | copy the frame into 8-bit `GL_RGB` and draw it back | Clamps the copied region to 1.0 (no headroom there); same as vanilla under clamp |
| MSAA resolve | averages unclamped samples | Edges of overbright regions differ slightly from clip-then-average |
| SSAO composite (`DST_COLOR` multiply) | darkens unclamped values | Only with SSAO on; overbright pixels can stay above 1 after AO |
| Bloom (`p2/emissive`, merged after these runs) | copies the frame into an `RGBA8` texture, bright pass on that | Not measured with FP16. The copy clamps, so the threshold stays a 0..1 level; with MSAA the copy is a multisample resolve into a different format (strict drivers may reject it). The composite is added before the final pass |
| Stereo / OpenXR | eye copies into the side-by-side buffer | The side-by-side buffer follows the toggle and is tonemapped in the final pass; the copy into the OpenXR swapchain is a blit (clamp, no filmic) |
| Alpha test | reads the fragment's own alpha | Unaffected |
| HUD and text | drawn into the main target before the final pass | Go through the tonemap: unchanged under clamp, whites above the knee dimmed under filmic |

## Implications for the design
- FP16 with clamp is close to vanilla once the GS-emulation draws saturate: 15/16 inside the
  band, and what remains is the expected translucent-over-overbright difference at noon.
- Vanilla lighting already puts 13 to 34% of a daylight frame above 1.0 (the tfrag `×2`), up to
  3.6. Any curve with a shoulder therefore changes the faithful look in daylight, not only the
  new emissive and light terms. The planned "identity below a knee" is still a look change at
  noon; a knee closer to 1.0, or a shoulder that starts above 1.0, keeps more of the vanilla look.
- The task asked for an exposure parameter; the plan says "no exposure". Exposure exists as a
  tonemap option (default 1, neutral) and is not used by anything else.
- HDR output will need: the tonemap split into a scene-to-display stage and an encode stage
  (scRGB or PQ); `paper_white_nits` to place 1.0; the peak from the calibration screen; UI and
  HUD (already saturated here) composited at paper white; the 8-bit copies (distort, warp) made
  float if their regions should keep highlights; the OpenXR copy tonemapped explicitly instead
  of blitted.
